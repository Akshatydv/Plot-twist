# Journey 03 — Bir × Barot: photography provenance

Two sets, kept apart on purpose.

**Place photographs (12)** — anything the page shows *as* Bir, Billing or
Barot. All from **Wikimedia Commons**, all **Creative Commons** (BY 2.0,
BY 4.0 or BY-SA 4.0), each checked against its Commons title and description
to be **in Bir, Billing or Barot** — no other Himachal town stands in for them.

**Moment photographs (7)** — golden hour, the fires, the last night, the
road. Chosen for the moment rather than the place, on the site owner's
instruction, and never captioned or alt-texted as Bir or Barot. Listed in
their own table below. Two Dharamshala paragliding frames and one otherwise
good Bir shot with a burned-in watermark were rejected on exactly that basis.

## What the licences require — and where it's done

- **Attribution** (all three licences): author, source link and licence link,
  shown where the work is shown. Rendered on the page by `<PhotoCredits/>`
  at the foot of THE DETAILS, from `photos` in `src/content/bir.ts`. That
  list and this table must stay in step.
- **Share-alike** (BY-SA): applies to *adaptations* of the photo. Resizing
  and the page-wide CSS grade (`.bir-grade`) are presentation, not a new
  work — but any photo that is re-edited and republished as a file must go
  out under BY-SA too.

## Scene photographs — `public/photos/bir/more/`

Added so no screen on the page is illustration-only: the route out of the
city, the night sky and sunrise, the Bir strip's six cards, the flight's
edge, the evening words, the cloud and forest descent, the trek beats, the
Day 04 moments, the end and the final call. Moment photographs, like the
set above: chosen for what is happening, never labelled as Bir or Barot.

Found through Openverse; Flickr originals downloaded at their largest
public size. **No-derivatives (ND) licences were excluded**, because every
one of these is cropped and colour-graded on the page; four otherwise good
picks were dropped for that reason, and three for being panoramas too thin
to fill a screen. One (`View of Barot valley`, Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0, by
Harvinder Chandigarh) is Barot and is used as the route's last stop.
